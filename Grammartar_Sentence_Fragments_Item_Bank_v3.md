### Design principle

**Value:** Diagnostic, practice, verification, and mastery evidence are separate exposure pools. Diagnostic and mastery-reserve items should never appear during instruction.

### Difficulty 1

**Value:** Obvious structure; useful immediately after instruction.

### Difficulty 2

**Value:** Moderate modifiers and familiar structures.

### Difficulty 3

**Value:** Distracting phrases/clauses or close contrasts.

### Recommended selection

**Value:** ```text
Begin with unseen Diagnostic reserve items; use unseen Practice items for guided/independent work; then unseen Verification items before progression; protect Mastery reserve for formal evidence.
```

### Repeat rule

**Value:** ```text
Do not repeat a previously answered diagnostic, verification, or mastery item as fresh evidence. If the practice pool is exhausted, AI-generated items may supplement low-stakes practice, but not diagnostic classification or formal mastery evidence.
```

### Repeat rule

**Value:** ```text
Do not repeat a previously answered item as evidence of mastery. If the practice pool is exhausted, AI-generated items may supplement low-stakes practice, but not formal mastery evidence.
```

### Escalation rule

**Value:** After 5–7 unsuccessful attempts on the same skill despite targeted remediation, assess prerequisite skills rather than continuing an endless fragment drill.

### Diagnostic rule

**Value:** ```text
A typical student sees about 8–12 sentence diagnostic probes plus 1 contextual task. Use third probes/tie-breakers only when evidence conflicts. Diagnostic success can skip instruction but does not itself confer mastery.
```
